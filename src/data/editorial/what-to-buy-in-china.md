# What to Buy in China: Gifts, Useful Finds and Where to Shop

The best things to buy in China are the ones made or sourced locally that you can check before you pay: tea, silk goods, porcelain and tea ware, tailor-made clothing, everyday gadgets, regional food and sauces, practical cookware, domestic beauty brands and new Chinese-style clothing. Buy in shops that show prices and give receipts, test electronics before you leave the counter, and check your own country's rules for food, plant products and antiques before you pack.

Updated 12 November 2026. Price ranges supplied with the editorial draft are illustrative budgets, not independently verified shop quotes. Compare current prices before buying. Store availability and destination-country import rules should be checked before travel.

## Choose by purpose

| You want | Good starting points | Main thing to check |
|---|---|---|
| A gift for friends or family | Tea, silk scarves, small tea sets, fans and paper cuts, beauty sets | Quality grade, packaging, and whether your country allows the item |
| Something for yourself | Tailor-made clothing, cookware, sportswear, gadgets, new Chinese-style clothing | Fit, voltage and warranty |
| A souvenir that is easy to carry | Paper cuts, fans, calligraphy sets, small ceramics | Whether it is hand made or mass produced |
| A business gift for partners | Good tea, a boxed tea set, a well-made local product from the partner's region | Cultural fit and your home country's gift rules |

## Things worth buying

These are ideas, not a ranking. Check each against your luggage space, budget and home-country rules.

### 1. Tea

**Why.** Tea is easy to carry, widely sold, and suits almost every kind of gift.

**Where to start.** Specialist tea shops, department-store tea counters and dedicated tea markets. Tea sold at major tourist landmarks is often priced higher than in residential-area tea shops, so compare a few shops first.

**What to check.** Ask to taste before you buy. Compare the grade, harvest year and weight on the package, not just the box. Avoid any offer that starts with a free "tea ceremony" and ends with a high-pressure sale.

**Taking it home.** Rules differ by country. For example, US authorities say travellers may bring any quantity of products composed solely of tea leaves, provided they declare them. [USDA APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/traveling-with-ag-products/coffee-tea-honey-nuts-spices) Check your own country before you buy in bulk.

**Indicative price ranges per 500 g (November 2026)**
- Entry-level everyday loose-leaf tea: ¥80–¥200
- Mid-grade gifting quality with branded packaging: ¥300–¥800
- Premium single-origin or named-harvest tea (for example Longjing, Da Hong Pao): ¥1,000–¥3,000 and up

**Shops and markets to compare**
- **Shanghai:** compare specialist tea shops and department-store counters. For packaged food gifts, the First Food Store is among the Nanjing Road food shops covered by the [Shanghai government shopping guide](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-TrendyStores/20251208/740057e2491f468daf27548ac0fb2c4e.html); check its current tea selection before making a special trip.
- **Hangzhou:** compare specialist tea retailers and neighbourhood tea shops; confirm the origin and harvest information on the product rather than relying on the location of the shop.
- **Guangzhou:** the Fangcun tea-market area. Compare several shops and ask about tasting, minimum quantities and opening hours before visiting. See the [Fangcun visitor guide](https://guangzhouhandbook.com/shopping/fangcun-tea-market/) for planning context; confirm arrangements with the individual shop.

### 2. Silk and silk goods

**Why.** Scarves, pillowcases and small accessories are light and look good as gifts.

**Where to start.** Silk shops and department-store counters in silk-producing areas, and in Shanghai and Hangzhou.

**What to check.** Look at the label for fibre content. Appearance and touch alone cannot prove that fabric is silk. Satin describes a weave, not a fibre, and can be made from silk or synthetic fibres. Ask for the fibre composition on the label and receipt. Very low prices for large "100% silk" items are a warning sign.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Small plain silk scarf: ¥80–¥150
- Mid-weight printed silk scarf: ¥200–¥500
- High-grade long scarf or a pair of pure silk pillowcases: ¥600–¥1,500 and up

**Shops to compare**
- **Hangzhou:** Dujinsheng, a heritage silk-weaving brand founded in 1922. [V&A Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O130006/cong-zhong-yi-qun-hanging-du-jinsheng-silk/) Compare its prices with neighbourhood silk shops.
- **Shanghai:** department-store silk counters on Nanjing Road.

### 3. Porcelain and tea ware

**Why.** Cups, pots and small bowls are classic gifts and often easy to wrap.

**Where to start.** Department stores, museum shops and specialist stores. Jingdezhen is known for porcelain and Yixing for clay teapots.

**What to check.** Look for chips and uneven glaze, and ask the shop to pack the item for travel. Most specialist shops provide protective packaging, sometimes for a small fee. Do not buy items sold as antiques unless you understand the export rules below.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Basic mass-produced tea set for four: ¥100–¥300
- Mid-grade hand-painted ceramic set or entry-level Yixing clay teapot: ¥400–¥1,000
- Premium artisan porcelain or branded Yixing collector ware: ¥1,200–¥3,000 and up

### 4. Tailor-made clothing

**Why.** Made-to-measure shirts, dresses and suits are a popular visitor purchase, especially when there is enough time for fittings.

**Where to start.** Shanghai’s former South Bund fabric market is now called the South Bund Custom Tailoring Center, following its renovation. [Shanghai government update](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-ShanghaiStyle/20261029/3a0fb8af9e344e23aae02ff6d768f4fd.html) Compare tailors and fabric samples before ordering. Tailor shops with fixed prices and written orders are an alternative.

**What to check.** Ask the lead time, fittings and what happens if the fit is wrong. Bring a garment you like as a reference. Get a written order slip showing fabric type, measurements, pickup date and deposit before you pay. Chinese clothing, shoe and ring sizes use different numbering from EU, US and UK sizes, so measure yourself instead of relying on a size label.

**Indicative turnaround and prices (November 2026)**
- Men's cotton shirt: ¥150–¥300 entry level; ¥300–¥600 for premium cotton
- Two-piece men's suit: ¥800–¥1,500 entry level; ¥1,800–¥3,500 for wool blends
- Turnaround and fittings: confirm both in writing with the tailor; do not assume a fitting or alterations are included in the quoted price.

**Tip.** Place the order at the start of your trip, not the day before you leave, so that there is time for a fitting. Fabric markets in general are covered in [China wholesale markets by product](/guides/china-wholesale-markets/).

### 5. Gadgets and consumer electronics

**Why.** China is a major electronics manufacturer, and Shenzhen's Huaqiangbei is one of the best-known electronics districts. Brand stores for domestic phone and camera brands are common in big cities.

**Where to start.** Brand stores and large electronics retailers for warranty-backed items. Huaqiangbei for accessories and small components. See [China wholesale markets by product](/guides/china-wholesale-markets/) for how to use it.

**What to check.** Test the item in the shop. Ask about the warranty and whether it works in your country. For use after you return home, check the input-voltage and frequency label against your destination’s electricity supply. A plug adapter changes the connection, not the voltage. Also check software region, app availability and network compatibility. Small accessories are often cheaper in Huaqiangbei than in brand stores, but quality varies, so test every item before you leave the stall. For phones and branded electronics with a warranty, use official retail stores, not street sellers. Be wary of "name brand" goods at unrealistic prices, which are often counterfeit or refurbished.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Small accessories (cables, chargers, phone cases): ¥20–¥100
- Mid-range portable speakers, power banks, compact home gadgets: ¥100–¥500
- Domestic-brand smartphones, cameras, premium electronics: ¥500–¥2,000 and up

### 6. Designer toys and collectibles

**Why.** Blind-box figures and collectibles from Chinese brands are a popular buy for younger people and collectors.

**Where to start.** Official brand stores and shopping-mall shops.

**What to check.** Buy from official stores to avoid fakes. Prices and availability vary by series and shop. Treat this as a casual browse rather than a must-buy. Some lines are also sold outside China.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Standard blind-box figures: ¥59–¥99 each
- Full series sets or mid-tier collectibles: ¥200–¥500
- Limited-edition or resale-premium figures: ¥800–¥2,000 and up

### 7. Regional food, sauces and snacks

**Why.** Sauces, spices and sealed snacks give a taste of a region.

**Where to start.** Local supermarkets, department-store food halls and specialist shops. Supermarkets show fixed prices, while tourist-area souvenir shops often charge more for similar gift boxes.

**What to check.** Buy factory-sealed, labelled and shelf-stable items. Many countries restrict meat, dairy, fresh produce and some plant products, and you may have to declare food at the border. US border officials, for instance, require travellers to declare all agricultural products, and prohibited items can be confiscated. [CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/travel/clearing-cbp/bringing-agricultural-products-united-states) Check your own country's list before you buy.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Single jars of sauce or individual snack packs: ¥20–¥80
- Mid-range gift boxes (sauces, dried goods, local treats): ¥100–¥300
- Premium specialty gift sets (check every ingredient against import restrictions): ¥300–¥500 and up

### 8. Cookware and kitchen tools

**Why.** Carbon-steel woks, bamboo steamers, cleavers and clay pots are practical and often good value.

**Where to start.** Kitchenware shops and department stores.

**What to check.** Weight and luggage rules. Knives and cleavers are not allowed in carry-on bags, so pack them in checked baggage, and check your airline's weight limits because heavy steel and clay items add up quickly.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Basic carbon-steel wok, bamboo steamer, small hand tools: ¥80–¥200
- Mid-range hand-hammered wok, full cleaver set: ¥250–¥600
- Premium artisan cookware, clay pot sets: ¥700–¥1,500 and up

### 9. Domestic sportswear and outdoor brands

**Why.** Chinese sportswear brands are common in malls, and some visitors buy shoes and apparel from them. Prices in China are often lower than overseas prices, but compare before you buy.

**Where to start.** Brand stores in shopping malls.

**What to check.** Sizes run differently and often smaller than European or US sizes, so try items on. Buy from official brand stores and keep the receipt.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Basic T-shirts, socks, small accessories: ¥80–¥200
- Mid-range running shoes, hoodies, training wear: ¥300–¥700
- Premium outdoor gear, technical jackets: ¥800–¥1,500 and up

### 10. Crafts, games and writing sets

**Why.** Paper cuts and fans are easy-to-carry gifts. Calligraphy kits and mahjong sets offer more substantial options, but can be heavy or bulky.

**Where to start.** Museum shops and craft shops.

**What to check.** Ask whether an item is handmade. Handmade pieces usually cost several times more than machine-made ones. Check the packaging of calligraphy sets and the weight of mahjong sets before you pack them.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Mass-produced paper cuts, folding fans, small souvenirs: ¥20–¥80
- Mid-range handmade crafts, basic calligraphy sets: ¥100–¥300
- Premium hand-carved pieces, full mahjong sets, artisan calligraphy kits: ¥400–¥1,000 and up

### 11. Domestic beauty and skincare

**Why.** Chinese skincare and makeup brands are popular with visitors. Lipsticks, sheet masks, small skincare items and gift sets are light and widely available.

**Where to start.** Cosmetics chains in shopping malls, department-store beauty counters and official brand stores. Major cities have multi-brand beauty stores, some with English-speaking staff.

**What to check.** Check the ingredient list if you have sensitive skin. Testers may be labelled only in Chinese. Liquids and creams over 100 ml usually have to go in checked baggage on international flights, so follow your airline's rules. Rules on bringing cosmetics home vary by country.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Single lipsticks, sheet masks, small skincare items: ¥50–¥200
- Mid-range serums, foundations, skincare sets: ¥200–¥500
- Premium gift sets and luxury domestic beauty lines: ¥500–¥1,000 and up

### 12. Modern hanfu and new Chinese-style clothing

**Why.** Modernised hanfu, horse-face skirts (mamianqun) and new Chinese-style tops and dresses are popular with visitors. Ready-to-wear pieces are easy to pack, and some shops offer made-to-measure options.

**Where to start.** Look for dedicated hanfu or new Chinese-style clothing shops in the city you are visiting. In Hangzhou, Suzhou or Guangzhou, confirm the current store location, size range and whether the pieces are ready-to-wear before adding a stop to your itinerary.

**What to check.** Sizing varies widely between brands, so try on ready-to-wear pieces if you can. Check the fabric content. Follow the care label; embroidery, trims and blended fabrics may need special handling.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Entry-level ready-to-wear skirt or top: ¥150–¥300
- Mid-quality full outfit: ¥300–¥800
- Premium embroidered or made-to-measure pieces: ¥800–¥2,000 and up

### 13. Desktop smart-home gadgets

**Why.** China makes a large share of the world's small desk and smart-home gadgets: LED desk lamps, portable humidifiers, desktop vacuum cleaners and magnetic phone stands. They are small, light and practical for personal use or casual gifts.

**Where to start.** Electronics malls such as Huaqiangbei, department-store home sections and variety gadget shops in malls. For a wider range at lower prices, Yiwu's International Trade City lists small appliances, telecom devices and electronic instruments in District 2, though stalls there often sell by the dozen or carton. See [Yiwu Market Map and District Guide](/guides/yiwu-market-map/).

**What to check.** Check the USB power requirements and included charger. For mains-powered products, match the input rating to the electricity supply where you will use them; a plug adapter does not convert voltage. Smart devices may also require a compatible app or region setting. Test every item in the shop.

**Indicative price ranges (November 2026)**
- Simple desk accessories, cable organisers: ¥20–¥100
- Mid-range smart lamps, humidifiers, small gadgets: ¥100–¥300
- Premium desktop appliances and smart-home devices: ¥300–¥800 and up

## Tax refund

Overseas visitors can claim a refund of value-added tax on eligible goods bought at designated tax-refund stores when they leave the mainland through a designated port. [Shanghai government guide](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Individuals-ShoppingandTaxRefund-Departuretaxrefund/20260813/469b30ac4ff348b8ac3a1259f0e12d23.html)

- Spend at least 200 yuan at the same store on the same day. The threshold was lowered in 2025. [MOFCOM notice](https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/gnmygl/art/2025/art_052eff0eb09e4d7cbda3691dbf5570fa.html)
- Leave the mainland within 183 days of your latest entry and within 90 days of the purchase.
- The goods must be unused and carried by you or in your checked baggage when you depart.
- Show your passport at the store and ask for the invoice and refund application form. Keep both.
- The refund is paid in renminbi. The applicable rate is 11% or 8%, less a 2% handling fee, so the actual refund is generally about 9% or 6% of the invoice amount.
- Refunds up to 20,000 yuan can be paid in cash or by bank transfer, and larger refunds by bank transfer only.
- Purchases of less than 10,000 yuan are randomly inspected at the port, and purchases of 10,000 yuan or more are inspected individually, so keep the goods easy to reach.

Ask the store whether it is a designated tax-refund shop before you pay. Rules are updated from time to time, so check the current guidance.

## Payment and bargaining

Foreign visitors can link eligible overseas bank cards to Alipay or Weixin Pay. Shanghai government guidance says a linked overseas card is generally exempt from the platform fee at 200 yuan or less, while a payment above 200 yuan may carry a 3% fee on the full amount. [Shanghai government payment guide](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-Individuals-BankingandPayments-Payments/20260921/c2c94d06a0454c8fa05386b80d0ae865.html) Check the fee on the app's confirmation screen.

Foreign-issued cards can also be used directly at merchants that display the matching network logo. Acceptance is not universal. Hotels, airports and larger chains are more likely to accept them, while smaller shops and market stalls mostly use QR payments. Carry some cash as a backup.

For setup, see the [official guide to mobile payments in Shanghai](https://english.shanghai.gov.cn/en-PaymentsInShanghai/20260920/f22b7e0f606c407e9da9d26b089eac48.html).

### Practical shopping tips

**Bargaining**
- Fixed-price stores, malls and supermarkets do not bargain.
- At markets, prices are often negotiable. Guides commonly suggest starting well below the first quoted price and working up gradually.
- The most reliable approach is to compare the same item at two or three stalls first. You will quickly learn the going price.

**Common tourist traps**
- **The free tea tasting.** Street promoters near tourist sites offer a free tea tasting or "ceremony", then pressure visitors to buy overpriced tea. Decline politely and walk away.
- **"Antiques" at street stalls.** Most "old porcelain" and "vintage jade" sold at tourist stalls is modern reproduction. Do not buy it as genuine.
- **Unrealistic brand discounts.** A branded bag, watch or sneaker at a fraction of retail price is likely counterfeit, and customs at home may seize it.

**Timing**
- Markets and independent shops tend to be busier at weekends. A weekday morning usually gives more attention from sellers.
- Malls often run promotions during national holidays, but tourist areas are much more crowded.

## Before you buy: fakes, antiques and carry-on rules

- **Fakes.** Imitation brand goods infringe trademarks and may be seized at your home border. Buy branded goods from official stores.
- **Antiques.** China restricts the export of cultural relics. Official customs guidance lists valuable cultural relics and other prohibited relics among items that cannot be exported. [China Customs](https://english.www.gov.cn/services/visitChina/202008/04/content_WS5f2905dec6d029c1c26372f0.html) Third-party guides give different age cut-offs, so do not rely on them. Buy new reproductions with an invoice, and avoid pieces sold as old.
- **Electronics and power banks.** Check destination voltage, plug type and warranty. Keep power banks out of checked baggage and check your airline’s current capacity and quantity limits. On domestic flights in China, power banks without a clear CCC mark, or from recalled models or batches, are prohibited. [CAAC notice](https://www.caac.gov.cn/English/News/202507/t20250709_227894.html) [Airline battery guidance](https://global.ceair.com/global/en_USD/Announcement/ScheduledGuidelines/boardingCheckin/safetyRules/index.html)
- **Food and plants.** Declare agricultural products and check your country's list before you buy.
- **Fragile items.** Ask the shop to pack them for travel, and keep receipts.
- **Liquids and sharp tools.** Check cabin liquid-container limits and restrictions on knives or cleavers. Use checked baggage only for items permitted there, and check every airline and transit airport on your route.
- **Shipping home.** Some large markets and shops have courier desks. For bulky, heavy or fragile items, ask whether they can ship to your home address, which can sometimes cost less than excess baggage fees. Get a tracking number and confirm the total price first.
- **Sizes.** Chinese clothing, shoe and ring sizes differ from EU, US and UK systems. Try items on or measure yourself before ordering made-to-measure pieces.
- **Receipts.** Keep receipts for tax-refund purchases, high-value items and anything you may return. They also help if customs asks you to prove the value of goods.

## FAQ

**Is shopping in China cheap?**
It depends on the item. Domestic brands and everyday goods are often good value, while imported branded goods can cost the same as elsewhere or more. Compare prices before you buy.

**Can I bargain?**
Not in department stores or brand shops. In markets, prices are often negotiable. Ask the price first, compare stalls, and walk away if it does not feel right.

**What should I not bring home?**
It depends on your country. Check restrictions on meat, dairy, fresh produce, plants, medicines, cosmetics and antiques before you travel.

**Do I need cash?**
Mobile payment is widely used, but foreign-card acceptance varies. Carry some cash as a backup.

**Can I get a tax refund?**
Possibly. You need to buy from a designated tax-refund store, spend at least 200 yuan there on one day, and leave through a designated port within the time limits above.
