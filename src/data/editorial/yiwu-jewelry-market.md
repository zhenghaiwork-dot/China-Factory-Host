# Yiwu Jewelry Market: A Buyer Preparation Guide

Start with District 1 of Yiwu International Trade City if you want to compare fashion jewelry, hair accessories or jewelry-making components. This guide focuses on three sourcing priorities: fashion alloy jewelry, stainless steel jewelry, and jewelry findings and components.

*About the examples: Local trading observations and November 2026 supplier examples in this guide were provided for editorial use by ChinaFactoryHost. They are practical planning references, not independently audited market statistics or guaranteed offers. Product materials and coating descriptions are supplier-stated unless otherwise indicated. Confirm the final quotation and order terms before paying.*

For buyers researching Yiwu fashion jewelry wholesale, the first decision is whether to buy available stock, modify an existing design or develop a new product. Those are different buying routes, with different quantities, prices and follow-up.

You do not need to turn every first conversation into a technical audit. You do need to know what the quotation covers, what you are accepting as a supplier claim and what must be confirmed before you sell the product to your customers.

## 2. What Can You Source?

This guide prioritizes alloy fashion jewelry, stainless steel pieces and components. Related categories can be useful for building a coordinated retail range.

| Product | Examples | What to record |
|---|---|---|
| Alloy fashion jewelry | Enamel earrings, pendants, charm bracelets, statement pieces | Body metal, posts or clasps, finish, weight and sale unit |
| Stainless steel jewelry | Hoops, chains, rings, bracelets | Which components are steel, specified grade, dimensions and finish |
| Findings and components | Hooks, backs, jump rings, clasps, connectors | Material, dimensions, wire thickness, hole size and pack quantity |
| Beads and decorative parts | Glass, acrylic, pearl-like and stone-described beads | Actual material, treatments, size, hole diameter and quantity per strand or bag |
| Hair accessories | Claws, clips, pins, headbands | Body material, spring or clip mechanism, edges and attachment strength |
| Packaging | Cards, bags, pouches, boxes and labels | Size, design, quantity, printing and packing labour |

Finished jewelry and components involve different work after purchase. Buying loose parts cheaply does not necessarily save money once assembly, reject sorting and packaging are included.

Fashion jewelry also does not mean counterfeit jewelry. Build your range around products you have a right to sell, rather than copied logos or a recognizable branded design offered without authorization.

## 3. Choose Your Buying Route

### Ready stock

Existing stock is usually the easiest starting point for a trial. Ask what is available now, how it is packed and whether the minimum applies per style, color or size.

Do not assume everything on the wall is in stock. A display may represent an available batch, a previous design or a production option.

The following examples reflect the November 2026 District 1 supplier feedback supplied for this guide. They illustrate workable negotiation starting points, not market-wide entitlements.

| Supplier type | Example stock minimum | Mixed-order example | Sample arrangement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast-fashion alloy jewelry | One dozen per style and color | Five dozen total across three or more styles | Twice the bulk unit price; credit against an order above ten dozen where agreed |
| Basic stainless steel styles | 50 sale units per style and size | 100 sale units across two or more styles | Retail-priced samples; not refundable for small trials |
| Components and findings | One pack or one gross, depending on the item | RMB 200–500 total order value | Small quantities at a marked-up price |

One gross means 144 pieces. For finished earrings, confirm whether a quoted quantity means individual earrings or pairs. “100 pieces” can otherwise create an expensive misunderstanding.

Display pieces are not always for sale. If you need one or two reference items, ask for a paid sample rather than assuming the booth offers general retail purchasing.

### Modifying an existing product

Changes can include a different earring post, chain length, finish, pendant, card or bag. Ask which change creates the minimum quantity: the product, coating batch, printed packaging or assembly work.

A low product MOQ does not automatically apply to a custom finish or printed card.

### New development

New shapes, molds or structural changes need a clearer brief. Agree on sample approval, development costs, tooling ownership, production timing and what happens if the sample does not meet the brief.

The seller may coordinate more than one workshop. That can be workable, but responsibility for the finished item and any change in specification should be clear.

## 4. Yiwu Jewelry Prices and MOQ: Compare Like with Like

Ask the seller to separate four figures:

1. Sample quantity and sample price;
2. Existing-stock minimum per style, color and size;
3. Production or modification minimum;
4. Packaging minimum and extra charges.

A low unit price is not useful if it requires more inventory than you can sell.

### Useful negotiation questions

- Can I mix colors within this quantity?
- Can I combine styles to reach the total order minimum?
- What is the price for a smaller trial?
- Does standard packaging reduce the minimum?
- Is the same style available for a repeat order?

A supplier can agree to one request and reject another. Explain your real trial quantity instead of promising a large future order merely to obtain a lower price.

## 5. Materials and Finishes: Ask About the Whole Item

A material name may describe only one component. For earrings, separate the body, post and back. For necklaces, distinguish the chain, pendant, clasp and connecting rings.

| Description | What to clarify |
|---|---|
| Alloy | The intended base metal or composition and consistency on repeat orders |
| Stainless steel | Which components are steel, which grade is specified and whether other metals are used |
| 925 silver | Which components carry the claim and what evidence supports it |
| Gold plated / 18K plated | Base metal, coating specification, process and parts covered |
| PVD / vacuum plated | What process and coating the seller means, not just the marketing term |
| Natural stone / crystal / pearl | Whether natural, cultured, synthetic, treated or imitation |

The [FTC’s jewelry guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-platinum-gold-and-silver-jewelry) explains the distinction between precious metal and plating. A gold-colored surface does not establish solid-gold content, and a stamped description is not independent material verification.

### Common supplier claims and follow-up questions

| What the seller says | What to ask |
|---|---|
| “The color lasts six months.” | Does that mean dry wear only? What about showers, sweat or seaside use? Is there supporting test information? |
| “Stainless steel, hypoallergenic.” | Are the body, posts, backs and clasps the same material? What supports the hypoallergenic claim? |
| “18K real gold plating.” | What is underneath? What coating thickness is specified? Is the process electroplating or PVD? |
| “Waterproof and tarnish-free.” | What conditions and exposure duration does that cover? Is there a relevant performance report? |

### Bilingual conversation example

The following is an illustrative conversation supplied for this guide, not a recorded interview or a statement of standard coating thickness.

> Supplier: 这个是真空电镀的，正常戴保色半年没问题。  
> “This is vacuum plated. It keeps its color for six months with normal wear.”
>
> Buyer: 保色是指日常不碰水吗？海边佩戴会不会掉色很快？镀层厚度大概是多少？  
> “Does that mean no water contact? Would seaside wear make it fade faster? What is the approximate coating thickness?”
>
> Supplier: 最好别碰水碰汗，海边肯定快一点。常规厚度大概0.03微米。  
> “It is better to avoid water and sweat. It fades faster at the beach. Our standard coating is around 0.03 microns.”

The useful result is the narrower claim: the seller is advising against conditions that many customers consider normal wear. The thickness remains a supplier statement about that example—not an established PVD specification or proof of durability.

Do not repeat “hypoallergenic,” “waterproof” or “tarnish-free” in your own listings solely because a seller used the phrase.

## 6. Samples and Quotations: Keep the Records Connected

For shortlisted products, check pairs match, posts are straight, backs and clasps fit, stones are secure and surfaces have no obvious sharp edges, glue residue or scratches. Compare lengths, weights, colors and component dimensions where relevant.

These checks assess visible workmanship. They do not establish chemical composition or long-term wear performance.

Keep three stages distinct:

- Display sample: the piece that attracted your attention;
- Approved reference: the identified sample and specification accepted for the order;
- Supplied goods: the stock or production batch actually delivered.

A stock purchase may need only a short written confirmation. A custom order needs more detail. A WeChat discussion can start the process, but collect the final agreement in one readable order summary.

| Record | Include |
|---|---|
| Supplier | Contact, full booth address and agreed payment recipient |
| Product | Style code, photo reference and sale unit |
| Quantity | By style, color and size |
| Materials and finish | Separate components, coating description and approved reference |
| Packaging | Included packing, retail presentation and extra labour |
| Costs | Unit price, sample, printing, setup, delivery and applicable taxes |
| Timing | Stock status, preparation or production period and delivery point |
| Remedies | Shortage or defect reporting, evidence, deadline and agreed response |
| Reorders | Availability, minimums and notification of specification changes |

## 7. Common On-Site Pitfalls and Practical Fixes

These observations reflect local sourcing experience supplied by ChinaFactoryHost. The percentage ranges below are working estimates from that experience, not independently measured market averages. Use them as practical comparison prompts, not proof about an individual booth.

### 1. Retail-style quotations instead of bulk prices

A new visitor who asks only “How much?” may receive a single-item or sample-style price. Local sourcing experience supplied for this guide puts some differences around 30–50% above a comparable bulk quotation; the difference depends on the product and quantity.

Open with the buying basis:

- What is the price per dozen or per 100 sale units?
- What is the minimum per style and color?
- What are the next quantity breaks?

A useful Chinese opener is “这个怎么拿货？”—roughly, “What are the wholesale buying terms for this?” Follow with the exact quantity and sale unit. Knowing the phrase does not itself guarantee the lowest price.

### 2. Different supply layers and reseller markups

Not every booth is a factory. The local experience supplied for this guide includes reseller markups estimated at 30–80% over upstream purchase cost in some cases. Buyers generally cannot see that upstream cost, so this is a warning to compare—not a margin you can reliably calculate from the display.

A focused product range can be a useful lead when looking for a specialist supplier. A very broad range of unrelated goods can be a reason to ask more questions about sourcing. Neither proves ownership of a factory.

Compare three to five genuinely comparable offers. Check material, finish, quantity, packing and service before deciding a higher quotation is simply a middleman’s markup. A trader who handles useful small quantities or consolidation can still be the right supplier.

### 3. Display quality that differs from the supplied goods

Do not approve a large purchase solely from the wall sample. Ask to see actual stock and agree how a production batch will be checked.

Where inspection is permitted, check more than the top layer: select packages from different carton positions. Photograph the approved reference and link it to the booth and style code. Agree how defects and shortages will be handled before payment.

### 4. A low price tied to excessive quantities

Some attractive prices require quantities such as twelve dozen per color. Compare the total spend and inventory commitment, not just the unit price.

Ask about mixed colors, mixed styles or a smaller trial at a different price. Local buyers may see fast-fashion demand shift within a week or two; treat that as a reason to test unfamiliar designs, not a fixed shelf life for every style.

### 5. Vague promises about materials and color retention

“Six months” may exclude water and sweat. “Stainless steel” may refer only to one component. “Gold plated” does not tell you the base metal or thickness.

Use the questions in the materials section and put the relevant answers in the order record. Supplier descriptions should not become unqualified promises to your customers.

### 6. Weight-sold stock lots outside the main market

Local sourcing experience identifies weight-sold jewelry lots in peripheral stock-trading areas such as Beixiazhu and Zhucun. These are not the same purchasing environment as a normal District 1 stock order.

Packaging, heavy components, defects and unsellable designs can make a cheap kilogram price misleading. Inspect a representative selection with the seller’s permission and calculate cost per usable piece, not just gross weight.

Final-sale terms are common in this type of transaction according to the local experience supplied here. Confirm the actual terms before paying. Without experience sorting such lots, a first-time buyer is usually better served by identifiable styles with clear quantities and specifications.

### 7. Losing hours without a shortlist

Use product references and market directories to identify candidates before arriving. Four to six target booths is a useful starting plan, not a requirement to visit a fixed number.

Use image search where available, then check the match, current booth and contact. A similar photograph does not establish identical materials or current stock. The [Yiwugo market map](http://ditu.yiwugo.com/) offers shop lookup and location information; still keep the complete address saved.

Follow the overhead street and aisle signs and note your gate and floor. Do not rely solely on memory or a photograph of the product wall.

## 8. Packaging, Payment and Collection

### Packaging options and working minimums

The following tiers reflect packaging practices and example terms supplied by ChinaFactoryHost. Use them for budgeting and negotiation; final costs depend on the package, design, quantity and packing work.

| Option | Indicative extra cost | Working minimum / timing | What to confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard OPP bags | Often included in the base quotation | Usually no separate packaging MOQ | Individual or bulk packing, bag size and protection |
| Generic display cards | USD 0.03–0.08 per item | Around 100 per card design in the supplied examples | Available design, fitting and whether bagging is included |
| Custom cards, boxes or branded packaging | USD 0.10–0.25 per item in the supplied examples, plus any setup charge | Around 1,000 per design; allow an additional 3–7 days | Exact package type, printing, dimensions, assembly and final cost |

The custom range is a reference across supplied packaging examples, not a promise that a printed card and a rigid box cost the same. Request separate quotations for materially different packages.

Retail presentation and shipping protection are different jobs. A display card alone will not prevent bent posts, tangled chains or items rubbing in transit. Agree suitable bulk protection with the supplier and forwarder.

### Payment

Small stock purchases and samples may require full payment. Production orders may use a deposit and balance. There is no single appropriate payment split for every transaction.

Confirm what is ready, what still needs production, who receives the payment and when the balance is due. Resolve unexpected payment-recipient changes before transferring money.

### Collection across several suppliers

Keep goods linked to their supplier and order code. Confirm the receiving address, contact, carton marks, quantities and dispatch date.

Ask whether local delivery is included. A supplier’s ability to send goods to a hotel or warehouse does not establish free delivery or mean that storage and international shipment are arranged.

## 9. Reorders and Restocking Reliability

The following patterns reflect local supplier feedback supplied for this guide, not guaranteed replenishment commitments:

- Running-stock basics: Popular simple styles may be replenished within 3–7 days when stock or production capacity allows.
- Seasonal and fast-fashion designs: A sold-out batch may not return. Ask whether reproduction is available.
- Specification changes: Base metals, components or plating arrangements can change between batches. Request advance notification and written approval of substitutions.
- Repeat-order quantities: Established customers may negotiate the same or lower minimum than the first order; agree the quantity rather than assuming the concession.

Do not build a continuing range around the assumption that a display style will still exist three months later. For important lines, keep an identified reference sample and record the replenishment arrangement.

A signed or otherwise clearly acknowledged sample helps identify what was approved. It does not replace checking that later goods match it.

## 10. Product Safety and Your Destination Market

Commercial terms are negotiable; your responsibilities to customers depend on where and how the product is sold.

Identify the destination country and intended wearer before deciding what testing or documentation is needed. Children’s jewelry requires particular attention. The [US CPSC jewelry FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Jewelry) distinguishes adult-jewelry consensus standards from requirements applying to children’s products.

For the EU or another destination, ask a qualified laboratory or adviser which chemical, labeling and product requirements apply. A laboratory’s name on a document is not a universal approval for every item the supplier sells.

Check the report’s product description, materials, coating, sample identification, date and test scope. A report for another finish or product does not automatically support your order or marketing claims.

## 11. Plan a Focused First Visit

A useful first visit has a task, not just a destination.

1. Decide whether you need finished stock, components or development support.
2. Prepare product references, quantities and essential specifications.
3. Shortlist relevant booths and save the full addresses.
4. Compare a manageable set of products using consistent quote fields.
5. Label samples and complete notes during a break.
6. Revisit the strongest candidates to settle unanswered points.
7. Arrange collection and leave time for delays before your return journey.

Confirm your target suppliers will be present. Building opening hours do not guarantee every booth is staffed throughout the day. Ask before taking product or price photographs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the MOQ at the Yiwu jewelry market?

There is no single Yiwu jewelry market MOQ. Stock may be sold in dozens, larger style quantities, packs or gross units. Confirm the minimum per style, color and size, and ask whether mixed orders count toward it. Custom finishes and packaging can introduce additional minimums.

### Where should I start in the Yiwu jewelry accessories market?

Start with the category, not simply the word “accessories.” District 1 East’s first floor is useful for components; the main building’s second floor is a starting point for finished jewelry, and the third floor lists accessories among other categories. Check the specific supplier before travelling.

### Can I buy only one or two pieces?

Some suppliers sell paid samples; others will not sell the display piece. Ask the sample price, availability and whether any fee can be credited against a later order. Do not assume every booth offers retail service.

### Is jewelry from Yiwu made in Yiwu?

Not necessarily. A booth can represent local production, another manufacturing area or several partners. Ask who is responsible for the finished order and whether a production visit is relevant to your purchase.

### How should I prepare to buy jewelry from Yiwu, China?

Bring reference images, your intended quantity per style and color, destination market and material priorities. Decide whether you want ready stock or custom work, shortlist suppliers and allow time to revisit before ordering.

### Is stainless steel jewelry automatically hypoallergenic?

Do not treat the material label as a guarantee for every wearer or every component. Confirm the actual components and obtain appropriate evidence before using a hypoallergenic claim in your own marketing.

### Has the jewelry market moved to District 6?

The market directory lists jewelry in District 1 and fashion jewelry in District 6. Confirm the address of the supplier you intend to visit rather than assuming a complete relocation.

### Do I need an agent or interpreter?

Not for every purchase. Support can be useful for technical discussions, supplier appointments, sample records and follow-up. Agree the task, fee and scope so that “sourcing support” does not mean something different to each party.
