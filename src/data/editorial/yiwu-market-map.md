# Yiwu Market Map and District Guide

Start with your product category, choose the district and floor, then confirm the supplier’s booth and entrance. This guide brings together district maps, floor directories and shop photographs to help you plan a focused visit to Yiwu International Trade City.

Product-floor listings were checked against the Yiwugo directory on 12 November 2026. Supplied photographs and diagrams are reference material, not a guarantee of current shop locations. Confirm entrances, opening arrangements and transport locally before travelling.

## Overall market layout

Use the overview below to understand how Districts 1–5 relate to one another, including the east extensions of Districts 1 and 2. It does not show the newer Global Digital Trade Center, commonly listed as District 6. Search for that complex separately when arranging transport.

Choose one or two priority districts before you arrive. The number of shops you can assess depends on how long you spend discussing specifications, samples and order terms—not simply how quickly you walk through the buildings. Ask shortlisted suppliers for their district, floor, booth number, nearest entrance and a map pin.

## Read booth and street signs

A booth number alone is easy to misplace. Save the full address in a consistent format:

**District / building · floor · zone or street · booth number · nearest entrance**

## Three ways to plan a sourcing route

These are suggested category sequences, not timed or field-verified itineraries. Confirm appointments and travel between buildings before setting a schedule.

### Route A: Gifts and everyday consumer goods

Start in District 1 for toys and jewelry. If stationery is central to your brief, continue to District 3; if socks and daily necessities matter more, choose District 4 instead. Leave time to return to the strongest candidates. District 5’s imported-goods area is a separate option, rather than an essential extra stop.

### Route B: Hardware and industrial products

Begin with District 2’s second-floor hardware, electrical products and locks. Continue to third-floor appliances and kitchen hardware where relevant. Add the east extension or District 5’s auto-parts section only when you have specific suppliers to visit there.

### Route C: Samples and small quantities

Contact shops in your target category before visiting and ask whether they sell samples or mixed small orders. District 4’s directory includes a Tourist Shopping Center, and District 5 has an imported-goods area, but a location does not guarantee single-piece sales. Confirm price, minimum order and payment terms with each shop.

For gifts and purchases to take home in your luggage, the [China shopping guide](/guides/what-to-buy-in-china/) covers product ideas, payment and packing checks.

## Facilities and practical preparation

- **Food and breaks:** locate the nearest dining area when you arrive; do not assume every district has the same food-court floor.
- **Information and maps:** use entrance directories or ask a staffed information point for help locating a booth and the nearest accessible route.
- **Payments:** agree on accepted payment methods before placing a sample order. Keep the receipt and the supplier’s registered details.
- **Shipping:** confirm who collects the goods, where they are consolidated and what the quoted transport price includes.
- **Restrooms and lifts:** follow current signs and note the closest lift if you are carrying samples.
- **Phone and connectivity:** bring a power bank and save addresses and important images offline.

## Buyer checklist

1. **Confirm appointments and opening arrangements.** Check holiday notices and ask shortlisted shops when they will be open; do not plan around an unverified blanket Sunday-closure claim.
2. **Wear comfortable shoes and carry less.** Arrange sample collection where possible rather than carrying every item between districts.
3. **Prepare a product brief.** Include material, dimensions, target quantity, packaging and destination market.
4. **Ask comparable questions.** Record MOQ, price at the same quantity, lead time and the exact specification behind each quotation.
5. **Compare several suitable suppliers.** A lower price may reflect different materials, packaging or terms.
6. **Separate samples from production commitments.** Agree on a reference sample and written acceptance criteria before a larger order.
7. **Understand an intermediary’s role.** Ask how fees and supplier relationships work before accepting sourcing assistance.
8. **Keep a clear supplier record.** Save shop photos, booth addresses, contact details and follow-up questions together.

A market visit helps you shortlist products and contacts; it does not by itself establish who manufactures the goods. ## FAQ

**How many districts should I plan for?**
The directory lists Districts 1–5 and District 6, the Global Digital Trade Center. Start with the one or two districts most relevant to your product brief rather than trying to cover all of them in a day.

**Which district is best for a first visit?**
Choose by product: District 1 for toys and jewelry, District 2 for hardware and bags, District 3 for stationery and eyewear, District 4 for socks and daily necessities, and District 5 for imports and textiles. District 6 adds newer category groupings.

**Can I rely on a free shuttle between all districts?**
Confirm current routes and fares at the market. The supplied bus photographs do not establish which stops operate today or whether a particular connection is free.

**Can I buy a single piece?**
Ask the individual shop. Sample purchases, retail sales and wholesale minimums vary; a district or floor does not guarantee a particular order policy.

**What are the opening hours?**
Check current market notices and confirm directly with the shops you intend to visit, especially around holidays. This guide does not assume all vendors follow identical hours or close on Sundays.
